\section{Research Question}

\begin{quote}
How does a firm's pre-rupture equity entry mode affect the capital it exposes,
the time required to exit, and the share of that capital recovered after a
political rupture?
\end{quote}

\section{Introduction}

A foreign firm can operate through a wholly owned subsidiary or share ownership
with a local partner. This choice determines the parent's capital exposure and
whether another owner must participate in decisions concerning the operation.
The same differences may affect the time and financial terms of exit after a
political rupture.

Russia after February 2022 provides a setting in which these effects can be
studied after a common political shock. Using Orbis and transaction-level
verification, \citet{EvenettPisani2023} identify 1,404 EU- and G7-based firms
controlling 2,405 active Russian subsidiaries. Only 5--13\% completed an equity
divestment during the first nine months after the invasion. This difference
between announced withdrawals and completed transactions makes the duration
and recovery of exit economically relevant.

The model takes each firm's pre-rupture entry mode as given. Until exit is
completed, the operation continues to generate revenue and costs. Its
post-rupture value therefore depends on three components: the capital exposed,
the net cash flow generated during the delay, and the amount recovered at exit.
A wholly owned subsidiary generally exposes more of the parent's capital and
gives the parent full control over a transaction. A joint venture limits the
parent's equity exposure, but the local partner may affect both the time and
terms of exit.

I model exit as a continuous-time process because an ownership transfer may
require an eligible buyer, government approval, and several stages of
negotiation. A limited set of buyers can reduce the value of specialized assets
\citep{ShleiferVishny1992,KimKung2017}, while sanctions can interfere with
transactions and payments even when they do not prohibit them directly
\citep{CrozetHinz2020}. The model represents these conditions through the exit
rate, net cash flow during the delay, and recovery at exit.

Existing research on Russia examines withdrawal announcements, completed
divestments, and changes in foreign ownership
\citep{BalyukFedyk2023,EvenettPisani2023,WellhausenZhu2026}. I examine whether
the ownership structure established before the invasion predicts how much
capital is exposed, how long exit takes, and how much of that capital is
recovered. The analysis focuses on equity operations because historical
ownership and completed equity transactions can be observed. Contractual
arrangements, branches, direct exports, and post-rupture use of third-country
hubs are outside the baseline empirical scope because they require different
population and outcome data. These extensions and their data requirements are
set out separately in \texttt{docs/data\_requirements\_entry\_mode.md}.

\section{Formal Model}

\subsection{Pre-Rupture Entry Mode}

Before the rupture, a foreign parent operates through one of two equity modes:
\[
m\in\{W,J\},
\]
where $W$ denotes a wholly owned subsidiary and $J$ denotes a joint venture
with an unrelated local shareholder. The model takes this previously chosen
entry mode as given and does not assume that the firm anticipated the later
rupture.

Let $K_m$ denote the book value of the foreign parent's equity exposure
immediately before the rupture. For a wholly owned subsidiary, the parent owns
the full book value. For a joint venture, $K_J$ is the parent's ownership share
of the operation's book net assets. This definition is narrower than total
economic exposure, but it corresponds to the equity information available in
Orbis.

\subsection{Exit After the Rupture}

Suppose an unexpected political rupture occurs after the operation has been
established. While exit remains incomplete, the operation generates net cash
flow $x_m$ per year. This amount equals operating revenue minus operating,
legal, compliance, maintenance, and other continuing expenses. It may be
positive or negative.

Exit is completed at Poisson rate $\lambda_m$ and produces net
recovery $P_m$ for the foreign parent. The rate summarizes the arrival of an
eligible buyer and the completion of any required negotiation and approval.
The baseline model assumes that $\lambda_m$, $x_m$, and $P_m$ remain constant
while exit is incomplete. Let $r>0$ denote the annual discount
rate and let $V_m$ denote the value of the unresolved operation immediately
after the rupture. Then
\[
rV_m=x_m+\lambda_m(P_m-V_m),
\]
so
\[
V_m=\frac{x_m+\lambda_mP_m}{r+\lambda_m}.
\]

The left-hand side is the required annual return on an unresolved operation
worth $V_m$. The right-hand side is the net cash flow generated while waiting
plus the expected change in value when exit occurs.

The post-rupture loss is
\[
L_m=K_m-V_m.
\]
This is an economic loss conditional on the rupture. The expectation is over
the time at which exit is completed, not over whether the rupture occurs.

It is useful to express the loss relative to exposed capital. Define
\[
p_m=\frac{P_m}{K_m},
\qquad
\xi_m=\frac{x_m}{K_m}.
\]
The expected loss per dollar of exposure is
\[
\ell_m
=
\frac{L_m}{K_m}
=
1-\frac{\xi_m+\lambda_mp_m}{r+\lambda_m}.
\]

The realized counterpart is
\[
L_i^{\mathrm{real}}
=
K_i
-\int_0^{T_i}e^{-rt}x_i(t)\,dt
-e^{-rT_i}P_i.
\]
This expression discounts both the cash flow generated during the delay and
the recovery received at the exit date. Because $P_i$ is defined as net
recovery, transaction costs must not be added again.

\subsection{Comparative Results}

The value of the unresolved operation satisfies
\[
\frac{\partial V_m}{\partial x_m}
=
\frac{1}{r+\lambda_m}>0,
\qquad
\frac{\partial V_m}{\partial P_m}
=
\frac{\lambda_m}{r+\lambda_m}>0,
\]
and
\[
\frac{\partial V_m}{\partial\lambda_m}
=
\frac{P_m-V_m}{r+\lambda_m}.
\]

Higher net cash flow and greater recovery reduce the post-rupture loss. A higher
exit rate also reduces the loss when the recovery at exit exceeds the value of
continued waiting, $P_m>V_m$.

The model does not impose a fixed ranking between the two entry
modes. While a joint venture reduces the foreign parent's book exposure, the
local partner may also help identify a buyer and complete the transaction,
raising $\lambda_J$, or delay agreement and reduce it. Entry mode may therefore
affect $K_m$, $\lambda_m$, and $P_m$ in different directions. Their net effect
is an empirical question.

\section{Empirical Implications}

\subsection{Unit of Observation and Outcomes}

The unit of observation is a foreign parent--Russian equity operation present
on 24 February 2022. A wholly owned operation is one in which the foreign
parent and its affiliates own the entire equity interest. A joint venture is
one in which the foreign parent owns at least 25\% but less than 100\% and an
unrelated local shareholder owns part of the remaining equity.

The first outcome is the foreign parent's pre-rupture book exposure:
\[
K_i=s_iB_i,
\]
where $s_i$ is the parent's ownership share and $B_i$ is the operation's 2021
book net assets.

The second outcome is $T_i$, the number of days from 24 February 2022 to a
completed exit. An operation that has not completed an exit by the final sample
date contributes its observed time through that date. This outcome measures
time from the rupture to an observed exit; it does not distinguish firms that
did not attempt to exit from firms whose transactions were delayed.

The third outcome is reported recovery:
\[
q_i=\frac{R_i}{K_i},
\]
where $R_i$ is the transaction value reported by Bloomberg for a completed
deal. This is a gross recovery measure and is not identical to the model's net
recovery $P_i$. Net recovery is used only when the parent's financial
statements disclose proceeds after transaction costs and taxes.

\subsection{Specifications}

Capital exposure is estimated using
\[
\log K_i
=
\alpha_K+\beta_{KJ}J_i
+X_i'\gamma_K+\delta_{s(i)}+\delta_{h(i)}+\varepsilon_{Ki},
\]
where $J_i$ indicates a joint venture. The terms $\delta_{s(i)}$ and
$\delta_{h(i)}$ are sector and parent-home-country fixed effects.

Time to exit is estimated using a proportional-hazard specification:
\[
\lambda_i(t)
=
\lambda_0(t)
\exp\!\left(
\beta_{\lambda J}J_i
+X_i'\gamma_\lambda
+\delta_{s(i)}+\delta_{h(i)}
\right).
\]
Unlike the constant-rate benchmark in the formal model, the baseline hazard
$\lambda_0(t)$ may change over time. A piecewise-exponential specification can
also estimate separate rates for different periods after the rupture.

For completed exits with a disclosed transaction value, recovery is estimated
using
\[
q_i
=
\alpha_q+\beta_{qJ}J_i
+X_i'\gamma_q+\delta_{s(i)}+\delta_{h(i)}+\varepsilon_{qi}.
\]
This specification estimates recovery conditional on both exit completion and
value disclosure. It does not identify average recovery among all
pre-rupture operations. Completion and disclosure rates must therefore be
reported separately by entry mode.

When post-rupture subsidiary accounts are available, net cash flow is measured
as
\[
\xi_{it}
=
\frac{x_{it}}{K_i}
\]
and estimated using
\[
\xi_{it}
=
\alpha_x+\beta_{xJ}J_i
+X_i'\gamma_x+\eta_t
+\delta_{s(i)}+\delta_{h(i)}+\varepsilon_{xit},
\]
where $\eta_t$ denotes year effects. The sample includes only years in which
the operation remains unresolved.

The mode coefficients are conditional associations. A causal interpretation
would require entry mode to be unrelated to unobserved determinants of exit
outcomes after conditioning on $X_i$ and the fixed effects. Because firms
selected their entry modes before 2022, this condition is not assumed. A
within-parent specification can be reported for parents operating multiple
Russian entities under different ownership structures.

For calculations requiring a discount rate, the baseline uses $r=0.05$ per
year. Results should also be reported for alternative rates between zero and
10\%.
