FOREIGN-MARKET ENTRY MODE AND THE COST OF EXIT AFTER POLITICAL RUPTURE

Research proposal

1. Research question

Foreign firms commonly enter a market through a wholly owned operation, a
joint venture, or a contract with a local distributor, licensee, or
franchisee. The choice affects operating control, knowledge transfer, local
capital investment, and dependence on a local partner. This study asks how the
same choice affects the cost of leaving after relations between the home and
host countries deteriorate.

The empirical question is:

    How does a firm's pre-rupture entry mode affect its accounting loss, time
    to resolution, and recovery of invested capital after a political rupture?

The first setting is foreign business activity in Russia after 24 February
2022. Foreign firms using different entry modes faced the same change in the
political environment. The study will compare outcomes across those modes
while accounting for observable differences that affected the earlier entry
choice.

2. Contribution

Research on foreign-market entry explains how ownership and local partnerships
affect control, knowledge protection, adaptation, and operating performance.
Research on political risk explains how expropriation, sanctions, and policy
changes affect foreign investment. Less is known about whether the original
entry arrangement determines the cost of leaving after a severe political
shock.

The study makes three contributions. First, it treats exit cost as an outcome
of the earlier entry decision. Second, it measures exit cost with accounting
loss, duration, and recovery rather than an announcement that the firm intends
to leave. Third, it separates voluntary sale, contract termination, continued
ownership, liquidation, and state-imposed loss of control.

The result will be relevant beyond Russia if it identifies general components
of exposure: capital placed under host-country jurisdiction, dependence on a
local partner, the availability of an eligible buyer, and the legal process
needed to end the arrangement.

3. Theory

Before a rupture, firm i chooses entry mode m from wholly owned operation,
joint venture, and contractual entry. Its expected value is

    U_im = pi_im - I_im - p_i L_im.

pi_im is the value of normal operations, I_im is the cost of establishing the
arrangement, p_i is the perceived probability of a political rupture, and L_im
is the expected loss if a rupture occurs.

After a rupture, an unresolved operation has value

    V_im = [lambda_im P_im + sigma_im S_im - c_im]
           / [r + lambda_im + sigma_im].

K_im is the firm's pre-rupture capital exposure. lambda_im is the rate at which
a permitted resolution occurs, P_im is net recovery at that resolution, c_im
is the cost of maintaining the unresolved position, sigma_im is the rate of a
forced loss of control, and S_im is recovery after forced loss. Expected exit
loss is

    L_im = K_im - V_im.

Wholly owned operations usually place more assets under host-country
jurisdiction. Joint ventures divide ownership but may either help or impede
resolution. A local partner can identify a buyer and obtain approval, or can
block a transaction and demand a larger share of the remaining value.
Contractual entry usually places less equity at risk, but the foreign firm can
still lose receivables, inventory, guarantees, and control over licensed
technology or brands.

The model does not assume that one mode is always best. It gives three outcomes
to estimate:

    H1. Entry modes differ in accounting loss relative to pre-rupture exposure.

    H2. Entry modes differ in the time required to reach legal and operational
        resolution.

    H3. Entry modes differ in the fraction of pre-rupture exposure recovered by
        the foreign parent.

For wholly owned operations and joint ventures, the direction of the difference
is an empirical question. Contractual entry should have lower total loss when
its reduction in local capital exposure is larger than any unpaid receivables,
contract penalties, guarantees, and loss of licensed assets.

4. Unit of analysis and sample

The preferred unit is a distinct Russian business presence linked to a foreign
parent. A parent can have several units and can use more than one entry mode.
The population should include every foreign firm with documented Russian sales
or operations in 2021, including firms that continued operating after the
invasion.

The sample should not be limited to public exit announcements or reported
transactions. Those restrictions would exclude unresolved firms and many
contractual arrangements. The analysis period begins on 24 February 2022 and
continues through the most recent date for which ownership and financial
outcomes can be verified consistently.

5. Entry-mode measurement

Entry mode must be measured as of 31 December 2021.

    Wholly owned operation: the foreign parent controlled at least 95 percent
    of voting rights, directly or indirectly.

    Joint venture: the foreign parent held an equity interest below 95 percent
    and an unrelated partner held a material ownership or contractual control
    right.

    Contractual entry: the firm served Russia through a distributor, licensee,
    or franchisee and held no equity in the local operator.

A firm that used several modes will be divided into separate presence units
where the data permit. If only parent-level outcomes are available, the main
mode will be defined by the largest share of 2021 Russian revenue. Mixed-mode
firms will also be reported separately. Two researchers should code each case
from source documents and resolve disagreements using a written coding rule.

6. Outcome measurement

The study will measure three primary outcomes.

Accounting loss is the cumulative Russia-specific impairment, write-down,
deconsolidation loss, contract charge, and realized loss reported after the
rupture, divided by the firm's pre-rupture Russian exposure.

Time to resolution is the number of days from 24 February 2022 to the first
verified date on which ownership transferred, the entity was liquidated, the
contract ended, or the parent permanently lost control. Firms that remain
unresolved at the end of the sample remain in the analysis rather than being
assigned an artificial completion date.

Recovery is cash received plus the fair value of retained claims, royalties,
or repurchase rights, net of required contributions and transaction taxes,
divided by pre-rupture exposure. Nominal amounts will be converted to a common
currency using the exchange rate on the transaction date.

A secondary summary measure is

    Exit Cost_i = K_i - R_i + Holding Cost_i + Direct Transaction Cost_i.

This measure will be reported only when its components are observed. Missing
recovery or write-down information will not be replaced with zero.

7. Data

The ideal dataset requires five linked sources.

First, a historical Orbis or comparable ownership export will identify direct
and indirect shareholders, voting stakes, subsidiaries, 2021 assets, equity,
revenue, employment, profitability, and industry. Current ownership records
cannot substitute for historical ownership because an exit changes the field
being used to define entry mode.

Second, distributor, license, and franchise relationships will be collected
from annual reports, company websites archived before 2022, commercial
agreements, trade publications, and local operator disclosures. This source is
necessary because non-equity arrangements are generally absent from ownership
databases.

Third, Russian company-registry records will establish changes in legal owner,
liquidation, and current legal status. These records will distinguish an
announcement from a completed transfer.

Fourth, Bloomberg transaction records will provide buyer identity,
announcement and completion dates, stake size, status, and disclosed value.
Values will be checked against company filings and registry evidence.

Fifth, annual reports, regulatory filings, and earnings-call materials will
provide Russia-specific write-downs, impairment charges, proceeds, guarantees,
retained options, and continuing obligations.

The current repository contains a recent Orbis export, Yale action
descriptions, and 1,392 Bloomberg transaction records. These files are enough
to build a coding framework and a transaction baseline. They are not enough to
measure the three entry modes or exit cost for the full population.

8. Empirical design

The first analysis will report the distribution of each outcome by entry mode.
It will show sample size, missing-data rates, medians, means, and the full
distribution. Results will be presented separately for wholly owned
operations, joint ventures, contractual arrangements, and mixed cases.

For accounting loss and recovery, the baseline specification is

    Y_i = alpha + beta_J JointVenture_i + beta_C Contract_i
          + gamma' X_i + industry effects + home-country effects + error_i.

Y_i is either accounting loss divided by pre-rupture exposure or net recovery
divided by pre-rupture exposure. X_i includes 2021 assets, revenue,
profitability, employment, years in Russia, public-company status, whether the
sector was subject to foreign-ownership restrictions, and whether the parent
was directly sanctioned.

For time to resolution, the data will be arranged by firm and month. Among
firms that remain unresolved at the start of a month, the specification is

    Pr(Resolution_it = 1) =
        Logistic(alpha_t + beta_J JointVenture_i + beta_C Contract_i
                 + gamma' X_i).

alpha_t allows the general probability of resolution to change over time. The
analysis will also distinguish sale, liquidation, contract termination, and
forced loss of control because entry mode can affect those outcomes
differently.

Standard errors will account for multiple Russian units belonging to the same
parent. A secondary analysis will compare units within the same parent when a
parent used more than one entry mode. This removes stable parent
characteristics but not differences in the activities assigned to each mode.

9. Identification and interpretation

The rupture was common across firms, but entry mode was selected before the
rupture. Firms with regulated assets, complex production, valuable technology,
or a need for local political access may have chosen different modes. Those
characteristics can also affect exit cost.

The baseline coefficients are therefore conditional associations. Industry,
home-country, and pre-rupture operating controls will reduce observable
differences but will not prove causality. Within-parent comparisons provide a
stronger test when available. A causal estimate would require a credible source
of pre-rupture variation in entry mode that has no direct effect on exit cost.
The present proposal does not assume that legal ownership caps meet that
condition, because restricted sectors may face different treatment after the
rupture.

10. Preliminary result from current data

The current Bloomberg file contains 158 post-rupture transactions with a
foreign seller and a Russian target or Russian asset reference. Among the 145
transactions with a usable percentage interest, 57 of 114 transactions for at
least 95 percent of the interest were completed, compared with 22 of 31
transactions for smaller interests. The completion rates are 50.0 and 71.0
percent. The difference is 21.0 percentage points, with a two-sided exact-test
p-value of 0.043.

The median time from 24 February 2022 to recorded completion was 476 days for
full-interest transactions and 532.5 days for shared-interest transactions.
Transaction value was disclosed for 25 full-interest transactions and 8
shared-interest transactions.

This result does not test the proposed hypotheses. The interest sold is a
post-rupture transaction characteristic, not a verified pre-rupture entry
mode. Firms without reported transactions are excluded. Full-interest records
include asset sales, and contractual arrangements are largely absent. The
result is a description of the available Bloomberg sample and a check on the
data pipeline.

11. Required work

The next stage has four priorities.

    1. Obtain historical ownership as of 31 December 2021 and rebuild parent-
       subsidiary links without using post-exit ownership.

    2. Construct and double-code the distributor, license, and franchise
       population.

    3. Verify legal resolution dates and ownership changes in Russian registry
       records.

    4. Extract Russia-specific write-downs, recovery, guarantees, and continuing
       obligations from firm disclosures.

After these tasks, the study can estimate the outcome specifications and
assess whether the entry-mode comparisons are robust to sector composition,
firm size, and pre-rupture selection.
